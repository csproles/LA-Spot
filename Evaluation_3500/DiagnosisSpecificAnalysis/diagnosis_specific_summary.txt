EVALUATION_3500 — DIAGNOSIS-SPECIFIC ANALYSIS
================================================
Read-only join of the existing manifest, Evaluation_3500/full_run/results.csv,
and the ISIC source metadata.csv by isic_id. No YOLO/ABCD rerun, no mask,
threshold, or evaluation-policy change.

Population conventions (stated so nothing here is ambiguous):
  - "total_selected" = every manifest row in that diagnosis category
  - SINGLE_LESION_EVALUABLE / MULTI_LESION_AMBIGUOUS / NO_DETECTION = per-image
    evaluation_status counts, every image counted once
  - TP / FN / sensitivity: SINGLE_LESION_EVALUABLE only, per instruction
  - All other per-category stats (A/B/C mean/median, concern rates, mask
    area fraction, large_area_fraction rate, confidence) are ALSO restricted
    to SINGLE_LESION_EVALUABLE rows only, for internal consistency (one row
    per image, no double-counting from multi-instance images)

DOES THE AGGREGATE 33.1% REPRESENT MELANOMA PERFORMANCE?
------------------------------
No — it understates it. Melanoma-specific sensitivity is 40.2%, about 7
percentage points HIGHER than the reported aggregate. The aggregate is
pulled down toward basal cell carcinoma's performance (30.9%) because BCC
makes up 61% of the malignant sample (1069/1750) — more than twice the size
of the melanoma group (23%, 404/1750). Squamous cell carcinoma (31.4%) and
"other malignant" (25.5%, mostly keratoacanthoma) behave similarly to BCC,
reinforcing the same downward pull.

WHY MIGHT MELANOMA PERFORM BETTER? (associations only, not causal proof)
------------------------------
Two patterns line up with the sensitivity difference:
  1. A (asymmetry) concern fires much more often on melanoma (39.6%) than
     on BCC (25.7%), SCC (25.7%), or other malignant (21.8%) — consistent
     with melanomas classically being more irregularly shaped than the
     rounder, more nodular appearance typical of BCC/SCC/keratoacanthoma.
  2. The large_area_fraction rate (the "possible over-segmentation" quality
     flag directly implicated in false negatives by the earlier error
     analysis) is dramatically lower for melanoma (21.9%) than for BCC
     (53.1%), SCC (73.1%), or other malignant (70.9%).

A likely contributor to #2, found while checking this: the source
institution differs sharply by diagnosis type. 97% of BCC, 95% of SCC, and
98% of "other malignant" images come from the "MILK study team" contributor,
while melanoma images are split roughly evenly between "Memorial Sloan
Kettering Cancer Center" (54%) and "MILK study team" (46%). This suggests
at least part of the large-area-fraction gap — and therefore part of the
sensitivity gap — may reflect differing photography/framing conventions
between contributing institutions rather than a pure biological difference
in how irregular each cancer type looks. This is reported as an
association worth being aware of, not a proven cause, and no correction
for it has been made.

C (color) concern is uninformative for distinguishing these categories —
it fires on 83.5%-100% of cases in every single malignant category,
including the worst-performing ones, confirming (as in the earlier error
analysis) that color alone never differentiates a catch from a miss.

No thresholds were tuned and no pipeline changes are suggested based on
this analysis.
