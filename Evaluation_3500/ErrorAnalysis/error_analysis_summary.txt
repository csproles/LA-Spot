EVALUATION_3500 — POST-RUN ERROR ANALYSIS (TP vs FN, TN vs FP)
================================================================
Read-only analysis of the already-saved results at
Evaluation_3500/full_run/results.csv (the request referenced
"Evaluation_3500/results.csv", which does not exist as a path in this
project — the actual file lives one level deeper, at full_run/results.csv,
and that is what was analyzed). No pipeline component, mask, threshold, or
evaluation-policy code was touched or rerun. The only new computation is
reading already-saved per-instance mask PNGs to count lesion pixel area,
since the CSV only stores the relative lesion_fraction, not an absolute
pixel count.

Counts verified directly from the saved file before any analysis:
  TP = 440, FN = 891, TN = 997, FP = 308  — all four match exactly.

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHICH A/B/C FEATURES DIFFER MOST BETWEEN TP AND FN MALIGNANT LESIONS?
----------------------------------------------------------------
By far the largest, most consistent difference is in A (asymmetry):
  - TP median A = 0.243, FN median A = 0.104 (TP more than double)
  - A_concern fired on 81.6% of TP but only 3.0% of FN
This is also the largest effect size of any feature checked (Mann-Whitney
p < 0.0001, i.e. p reported as 0.0 to 6 decimal places).

B (circularity) also differs (TP median 0.374 vs FN median 0.233,
p < 0.0001), but B's concern flag almost never fires either way
(20.7% of TP, 0% of FN) — B's raw score differs, but it essentially never
crosses its own 0.50 threshold in this malignant subset, so it isn't doing
much work in the actual LOW/HIGH decision.

C (color) is the odd one out: its raw value is statistically different
(p = 0.000019) but in the OPPOSITE direction from A and B — FN cases have
a slightly HIGHER mean C value (0.528) than TP cases (0.490). C's concern
flag fires on almost everyone regardless of outcome (98.9% of TP, 89.5% of
FN) — color concern is nearly universal in this malignant sample, so on
its own it cannot distinguish caught cases from missed ones.

In plain terms: color alone flags almost every malignant lesion, correctly
caught or not. What actually separates a catch from a miss is whether
asymmetry ALSO flags concern.

----------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHICH CONCERN FLAGS ARE MOST OFTEN ABSENT IN FALSE NEGATIVES?
----------------------------------------------------------------
From the concern-combination breakdown (TP_vs_FN_concern_combinations.csv):
  - 89.5% of FN cases have ONLY the C (color) flag — nothing else.
  - Another 7.5% of FN cases have NO A/B/C concern at all.
  - Only 3.0% of FN cases have A alone, and 0% have B alone or any
    combination that includes A or B alongside C.
Compare to TP: 64.8% are A+C, 15.7% are A+B+C, 3.9% are B+C, 14.5% are C
only, and 0% have no concerns at all.

The scoring rule requires 2 or more flagged criteria (or a rare color
override) to reach HIGH. Since color alone is not enough, the single flag
missing most often in false negatives is A (asymmetry) — it is present in
96.9% of TP cases (A alone + every A-combination) but only 3.0% of FN cases.

----------------------------------------------------------------
3. ARE FALSE NEGATIVES ASSOCIATED WITH LOWER YOLO CONFIDENCE?
----------------------------------------------------------------
No. TP mean confidence = 0.617, FN mean confidence = 0.628 — FN is actually
very slightly HIGHER, not lower. Mann-Whitney p = 0.334 (not significant).
The low-confidence quality flag also shows no meaningful difference (30.2%
of TP vs 27.4% of FN, Fisher's exact p = 0.30).
This is a clear negative result: detection confidence is not what's driving
missed malignant cases in this dataset.

----------------------------------------------------------------
4. ARE FALSE NEGATIVES ASSOCIATED WITH LARGE/ABNORMAL MASKS OR OTHER
   SEGMENTATION-QUALITY FLAGS?
----------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, this is the strongest association found, and it lines up closely with
what A is doing:
  - mask area fraction: TP median 0.367 vs FN median 0.700 (FN masks cover
    roughly twice as much of the frame), p < 0.0001
  - large_area_fraction flag: 34.1% of TP vs 55.8% of FN, p < 0.0001
  - lesion_pixel_area: FN median (239,584 px) is almost exactly double the
    TP median (119,981 px), p < 0.0001
  - D_px (pixel diameter, informational only): FN median 698.7 vs TP median
    528.5, p < 0.0001 — the same "bigger mask" pattern shows up here too.

This is consistent with the segmentation investigation from earlier in this
project: an oversized, near-frame-filling mask tends to look more
circular/symmetric to the A and B math regardless of the lesion's true
shape, which would mechanically suppress A_concern — matching finding #1
and #2 above. This is reported as an ASSOCIATION, not a proven cause; this
analysis does not establish that oversized masks caused these specific
misses, only that the two are strongly correlated in this data.

One pattern runs the other way and should not be glossed over: FN cases
are actually LESS fragmented than TP cases (8.3% vs 26.8% flagged
fragmented; FN has fewer components on average, 1.20 vs 1.70). Fragmented
masks were shown earlier in this project to artificially inflate A — so it
is plausible that some TP cases are "helped" across the 2-flag threshold by
fragmentation-inflated asymmetry, while FN cases, being single coherent
(often oversized) blobs, do not get that inflation. This is a secondary,
associative observation, not a causal claim.

----------------------------------------------------------------
5. DOES THE EVIDENCE SUGGEST WEAK MALIGNANT SENSITIVITY IS MORE ABOUT
   ABCD BEHAVIOR, SEGMENTATION QUALITY, OR BOTH?
----------------------------------------------------------------
Both, and they are intertwined rather than separable in this data. The
proximate mechanism is an ABCD scoring behavior: color alone is not enough
to reach HIGH, and asymmetry is the criterion that decides most cases one
way or the other. But WHY asymmetry so often fails to fire on missed
malignant lesions is strongly associated with a segmentation-quality
pattern — oversized masks that mechanically read as more symmetric. Neither
factor cleanly explains the low sensitivity on its own; the pattern in the
data is that a segmentation characteristic (mask size) is associated with
an ABCD outcome (asymmetry concern), which in turn drives the final
LOW/HIGH decision. No pipeline change or threshold recommendation is being
made from this — this is a description of what's in the data.

----------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT PATTERNS APPEAR IN BENIGN TN vs FP CASES?
----------------------------------------------------------------
The general shape mirrors the malignant analysis in most respects: FP
(incorrectly flagged benign) cases have higher A (median 0.235 vs 0.128),
higher B (median 0.344 vs 0.225), higher C (median 0.405 vs 0.346), and
more A/B concern flags (A_concern 83.8% FP vs 7.7% TN; B_concern 14.0% FP
vs 0.2% TN) — all p < 0.0001. FP cases are also more often fragmented
(15.3% vs 4.5%) and slightly more often oversized (16.6% vs 11.8%, weaker
but still significant at p = 0.033 — a much smaller effect than the
55.8%-vs-34.1% gap seen for malignant FN).

One notable difference from the malignant side: confidence IS modestly
lower in FP than TN (0.710 vs 0.738, p = 0.025) — a real but weak effect,
the opposite of what was found for FN, where confidence showed no
association at all. Pixel area and D_px show no significant TN/FP
difference (p = 0.064 and p = 0.64 respectively), unlike the very strong
size effect seen on the malignant side.

In short: benign false alarms look like "too many ABCD flags fired,"
largely for the same asymmetry/border reasons as the true positives above
— these are lesions that legitimately look more irregular by the numbers,
not obviously a segmentation-artifact story to the same degree as the
malignant misses.

----------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHICH FINDINGS ARE DESCRIPTIVE VS STATISTICALLY SUPPORTED?
----------------------------------------------------------------
Statistically supported (Mann-Whitney or Fisher's exact p < 0.05, computed
directly on this saved data, reported in the CSVs):
  - A, B, C raw-value differences between TP/FN and TN/FP (all p < 0.0001,
    except TP-vs-FN C which is p = 0.00002 — still significant, smaller
    effect)
  - A_concern, B_concern, C_concern differences (all p < 0.0001)
  - mask area fraction and large_area_fraction flag differences for TP/FN
    (p < 0.0001) and TN/FP (p < 0.0001 for fraction, p = 0.033 for the flag)
  - lesion_pixel_area and D_px differences for TP/FN (p < 0.0001)
  - fragmentation differences for both comparisons (p < 0.0001)
  - confidence: NOT different for TP/FN (p = 0.33); WEAKLY different for
    TN/FP (p = 0.025)

Descriptive only (no causal claim, and in some cases no formal test
performed here beyond the association already listed above):
  - The specific mechanism ("oversized masks suppress asymmetry, which
    suppresses the HIGH decision") is a plausible explanation consistent
    with this project's earlier segmentation investigation, but this
    analysis only shows correlation between mask size and A/A_concern
    within the TP/FN groups — it does not prove that mechanism for any
    individual case.
  - The fragmentation-helps-TP observation (item 4, second paragraph) is
    explicitly a secondary, associative read of the numbers, not a tested
    causal hypothesis.
  - Any framing of "why" a specific lesion was missed beyond the
    statistical group differences above is interpretation, not a
    per-case finding.

No pipeline component, threshold, or scoring rule was changed or
recommended for change based on this analysis.
