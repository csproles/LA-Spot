EVALUATION_3500 — MELANOMA-SPECIFIC TP vs FN FAILURE ANALYSIS
================================================================
Read-only analysis of the 333 SINGLE_LESION_EVALUABLE melanoma cases
(134 TP, 199 FN) — counts verified exactly against the request before
analysis began. No YOLO/ABCD/mask/threshold/policy change.

1. WHAT CONCERN COMBINATIONS PRODUCE THE 199 MELANOMA FALSE NEGATIVES?
------------------------------------------------------------------
  C only (color concern, nothing else):    147 / 199  (73.9%)
  No A/B/C concern at all:                  35 / 199  (17.6%)
  A only (asymmetry concern, nothing else): 17 / 199  (8.5%)
  Any combination with B, or A+C, A+B+C:     0 / 199  (0.0%)

Every FN has at most one concern flag — consistent with the scoring rule
(2+ needed for HIGH, absent a color override). Three-quarters of misses are
"color flagged it, nothing else did."

2. HOW MANY FN HAVE C CONCERN BUT NO A/B CONCERN?
------------------------------------------------------------------
147 / 199 (73.9%) — this is the single largest failure pattern by far.

3. IS A STILL THE MAIN DIFFERENCE BETWEEN MELANOMA TP AND FN?
------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, clearly, and more so than for malignancy overall:
  A_value:    TP mean 0.258 vs FN mean 0.144  (p<0.0001, rank-biserial r=-0.81)
  A_concern:  85.8% of TP vs 8.5% of FN        (Fisher p<0.0001, OR=64.8)
This is the largest effect size of any feature checked, larger than the
combined-malignant effect size seen earlier. B differs too (p<0.0001,
r=-0.67) but almost never fires either way (23.9% TP, 0% FN) so it isn't
doing the discriminating work. C differs statistically (p=0.0023) but the
effect is small (r=-0.20) and C fires on most cases regardless of outcome
(97.8% TP, 73.9% FN) — consistent with question 1/2 above.

4. IS large_area_fraction STILL ASSOCIATED WITH FN WITHIN MELANOMA ONLY?
------------------------------------------------------------------
No — and this is the most important correction to the earlier, broader
malignant-level finding. Within melanoma alone:
  large_area_fraction rate: 22.4% of TP vs 21.6% of FN (Fisher p=0.893)
  lesion_fraction (raw):    TP median 0.319 vs FN median 0.203 (p=0.082,
                             not significant at the conventional 0.05 level)
  lesion_pixel_area:        p=0.311 (not significant)
  D_px:                     p=0.921 (not significant)
The strong mask-size/FN association found in the all-malignant analysis was
being driven by basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma (which have
much higher large-area rates overall), not by melanoma. For melanoma
specifically, an oversized mask is not a meaningfully different risk factor
for being missed than for being caught.

One weaker, secondary pattern: fragmentation is somewhat more common in TP
(17.9%) than FN (9.5%), Fisher p=0.030, and TP lesions have marginally more
components on average (p=0.003-0.023, small effect sizes ~0.09-0.12) —
consistent with the earlier observation that fragmentation can inflate A
and incidentally help cross the 2-flag threshold. This is a weak,
secondary, associative pattern, not a primary driver.

5. DOES INSTITUTION/SOURCE REMAIN ASSOCIATED WITH TP/FN WITHIN MELANOMA?
------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, modestly:
  MILK study team:                        sensitivity 45.9% (78 TP / 92 FN)
  Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center:  sensitivity 34.4% (56 TP / 107 FN)
  chi-square p = 0.042 (significant at the conventional 0.05 level, but a
  modest effect, and only two source categories with unbalanced group sizes)
This runs counter to a naive assumption that a single specialized cancer
center's images would be "easier" — here the multi-site MILK study team
images show somewhat HIGHER melanoma sensitivity. Reported as an
association only; no explanation for the direction is established by this
analysis (imaging equipment, patient population, and lesion severity mix
could all differ between sources and were not examined here).

6. ARE INVASIVE AND IN-SITU MELANOMA FAILURE PATTERNS SIMILAR?
------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, closely:
  Melanoma Invasive:  sensitivity 41.3% (66 TP / 94 FN), A_mean TP=0.252 vs FN=0.145,
                       large_area rate TP=21.2% vs FN=23.4%
  Melanoma in situ:   sensitivity 40.7% (57 TP / 83 FN), A_mean TP=0.267 vs FN=0.151,
                       large_area rate TP=19.3% vs FN=19.3% (identical)
Both subtypes show the same core pattern: a large A gap between TP and FN,
and no meaningful large-area-fraction gap. Melanoma, NOS (n=28) shows a
larger large-area gap (45.5% TP vs 29.4% FN) but the sample is too small to
draw a reliable conclusion from. Melanoma metastasis (n=5, all but 0 FN) is
too small to interpret at all.

STATISTICAL VS DESCRIPTIVE
------------------------------------------------------------------
Statistically supported (p<0.05, computed directly on this data):
  - A raw value and A_concern differences (TP vs FN) — large effect
  - B raw value difference (but B_concern too rare to matter practically)
  - C raw value difference — small effect
  - fragmentation and component-count differences — small effects
  - institution/source association with sensitivity — modest effect,
    two-category comparison only
Not statistically supported within melanoma (despite being significant for
malignancy overall):
  - mask area fraction / large_area_fraction / pixel area / D_px — none
    reach conventional significance for melanoma specifically
Descriptive only, no test performed or no causal claim made:
  - invasive vs in-situ pattern similarity (based on side-by-side numbers,
    not a formal interaction test between subtype and TP/FN)
  - any explanation for WHY the institution effect runs in this direction

No pipeline component, threshold, or scoring rule was changed or
recommended for change based on this analysis.
